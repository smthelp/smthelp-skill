In one paragraph

What the SME5200 is, what it cleans and how

The Smart EMS SME5200 Pallet Cleaning Machine from Southern Machinery is a batch spray cleaner for wave-solder pallets and other SMT and THT production tooling. Operators load 20 - 40 fixtures, wave-solder pallets, carriers, reflow coolers, filters or chains into a rotating Ø1000 x H200 mm basket and press one button; the machine washes with heated water-based liquid from top, bottom and front spray bars (720° coverage), rinses with water and blows the parts dry with 90 °C hot air in a 30 - 40 minute cycle. Liquid and rinse water run in separate 80 L closed loops with filtration down to 20 µm, and every wetted part is SUS304 stainless steel.

How it works

Four steps from dirty pallet to dry pallet

Each step follows the SME5200 spec sheet and intro deck. The diagram underneath is a concept view of a daily cleaning routine, not a drawing of the machine.

Verified source
LOAD

Load the basket

Place 20 - 40 fixtures, pallets or oven parts in the Ø1000 x H200 mm rotating basket; a customised holder basket takes larger pallets.

WASH

Wash at temperature

The pump drives heated water-based liquid through a 20 µm filter to 3 spray bars - top, bottom and front - while the basket turns, giving 720° coverage.

RINSE

Rinse in a separate pipe system

Clean water from its own 80 L tank and pipe system rinses away loosened flux and liquid; both circuits recirculate and are filtered.

DRY

Air-knife and hot-air dry

3 air knives cut off the water, then hot air up to 90 °C dries the load. The machine stops and resets; parts come out ready to use.

Learn it in 30 minutes

A 30-minute study route for the technician who owns the cleaner

Adapted from Southern Machinery's learn-SMT-in-3-hours training method: one question per block and a completion check at the end of each. Values come from the SME5200 spec sheet and intro deck. It does not replace the SME5200 manual or the installer's training.

0 - 6 min: How spray cleaning works

Heated water-based liquid is sprayed from top, bottom and front bars while the basket turns, so every face of the part is reached (720°). Rinse water has its own 80 L tank and pipes; liquid and water never mix.

Check: explain why the rinse water does not dilute the cleaning agent.

6 - 12 min: Liquid and safety

Use water-based cleaning agents only; flammable or volatile solvents must not be used in a heated spray machine. Auto fill and drain, closed tanks and the Ø120 mm exhaust keep operators away from the agent.

Check: name the type of cleaning agent the SME5200 is built for.

12 - 18 min: Loading and a first cycle

Load 20 - 40 pieces in the Ø1000 x H200 mm basket, depending on size, so spray can reach every pocket. Set temperatures and wash, rinse and dry times on the English touch panel and press start.

Check: run one full wash, rinse and dry cycle.

18 - 25 min: Reading the result

Inspect the parts after the cycle: residue left in pockets usually points to loading, wash time or temperature; a pressure alarm on the wash or rinse line is the first sign of a clogged filter or nozzle.

Check: list three reasons a part can come out with residue.

25 - 30 min: Filters and care

The wash liquid passes a pump-protection screen, a particle screen and a 20 µm filter core. Filter cores are typically changed every 15 - 30 days, depending on load.

Check: find the filter bucket and name the three filter stages.

Fault-to-check sequence

Start with a safe state and stop at the first point you are not authorised for.

  1. Observed symptom: note the alarm, the load, the cycle settings and what the parts look like.
  2. Safe state: stop the cycle and let the machine finish its stop before opening it.
  3. Pressure alarm on the wash or rinse line: check the screens and the 20 µm filter core first.
  4. Residue on the parts: check nozzles, loading density, wash time and liquid temperature.
  5. Confirmed and authorised: clean or replace and re-run. Not confirmed: tag the machine and contact Southern Machinery service.

Eight notes on clean pallets and fixtures

Long-form notes for process engineers, maintenance leads and SMT managers who want tooling that comes back clean every time.

ARTICLE 01The Solder Defect That Started in the Wash Room

Bridging at wave often begins with a pallet that was never really clean.

Full articles are in English. The summary above carries the argument in your language.

Monday morning, wave line two: the same connector bridges on every fifth board. The process engineer adjusts the flux, then the preheat, then the conveyor speed. Nobody looks at the pallet, which went back on the line after a quick brush on Friday night.

Flux baked into pockets and hold-downs lifts the board a fraction of a millimetre and changes how solder wets the pins. The usual suspects - flux, temperature, solder alloy - get blamed and adjusted, and each adjustment costs a shift of trial boards. The real cause sits in the tooling, and it gets worse every cycle the pallet is not cleaned properly.

A rule that works without buying anything: before you touch a process parameter, swap in a clean pallet and run the same board. If the defect disappears, the pallet was the cause. Keep one known-clean pallet per product family as a reference, and treat residue in pockets as a process variable, not housekeeping.

The Smart EMS SME5200 Pallet Cleaning Machine makes the clean pallet the normal one. 20 - 40 fixtures go into the Ø1000 x H200 mm rotating basket; heated water-based liquid is sprayed from top, bottom and front bars (720°), a separate rinse circuit washes it off and 3 air knives plus hot air up to 90 °C dry the load in a 30 - 40 minute cycle. Spray reaches pockets a brush cannot, and nothing scratches the pallet.

Make it a habit: log which pallet ran each lot and when it was last cleaned. When a defect appears, the log tells you in a minute whether the tooling is a candidate, and the list of 'unexplained' defects gets shorter every month.

ARTICLE 02Brushes Wear Out Pallets Faster Than Solder Does

Contact cleaning opens tolerances; spray cleaning leaves the tooling as it was.

Full articles are in English. The summary above carries the argument in your language.

A maintenance technician holds up a composite-stone pallet against the light. The edges of the pockets are rounded, the hold-down springs are loose, and there are fine scratches where a wire brush did its job every week for a year.

Wire brushes and scrapers remove flux, but they also remove material. Scratched composite-stone and aluminium pallets hold residue faster, hold-down tolerances open, and the next board sits wrong. The tooling budget grows, and the cause is booked as normal wear.

The method: separate cleaning from contact. Anything that has to touch the pallet to clean it will change it. Soften the residue with heat and a suitable water-based agent, move it with spray, and keep brushes for the rare spot that really needs them.

The SME5200 cleans by spray only. 74 stainless steel nozzles on top, bottom and front spray bars wash the parts while the basket turns, the rinse water comes from its own 80 L tank and pipe system, and the load is dried by air knives and hot air. All sheet metal, spray bars, air knives and pipes are SUS304.

Make it a habit: measure one reference pocket on each pallet type every quarter and note the result. When tolerances stay put after spray cleaning, you have the evidence to stop buying spare pallet sets for wear.

ARTICLE 03Filter the Liquid and the Cleaning Agent Lasts

Dirty liquid thrown away is the hidden cost of an open tank.

Full articles are in English. The summary above carries the argument in your language.

At the end of the week the soak tank is brown, the smell reaches the next aisle and someone drains the cleaning agent into a drum. On Monday the tank is refilled and the cycle starts again.

An open tank has no way to separate dirt from liquid, so the whole volume is discarded when it looks dirty. The agent is paid for twice - once to buy it, once to dispose of it - and operators are exposed to it every time they dip a pallet.

The method: keep cleaning liquid and rinse water apart, and filter both. Remove particles before they reach the pump and the parts, and change filters on a schedule instead of changing the whole tank on a feeling.

The SME5200 keeps liquid and rinse water in separate 80 L tanks with their own pipe systems. Wash liquid passes a pump-protection screen, a particle screen and a 20 µm filter core before it reaches the spray bars, and the 5 filter cores are typically changed every 15 - 30 days depending on load. Fill and drain are automatic.

Make it a habit: record the date of every filter change and every liquid change. The ratio between them shows how well filtration is working, and a short interval tells you that loading or residue has changed.

ARTICLE 04Reflow Cooler Radiators Clean in One Cycle

Planned maintenance goes faster when the parts are washed in a batch, not soaked overnight.

Full articles are in English. The summary above carries the argument in your language.

A reflow oven is down for planned maintenance. The cooler radiators are thick with condensed flux, the flux filters are clogged and the technician's plan says 'soak overnight'. The line restarts a shift later than it could.

Oven parts are awkward: fins, chains, claws and nets trap flux in places a brush cannot reach, and overnight soaking ties up the parts and the tank. The downtime is counted against the oven, but it is really a cleaning problem.

The method: plan oven maintenance around the cleaning step. Remove the parts that collect flux, clean them as one batch while the rest of the maintenance runs, and refit them dry. A clean part ready on time is what shortens the stop.

The SME5200 basket takes reflow cooler radiators, flux filters, finger conveyors, claws, chains and nets. Before/after photos in the 2021 - 2023 SME5200 brochures show flux-coated cooler fins returned to bare metal. Wash, rinse and hot-air dry run in one 30 - 40 minute cycle, so the parts come back during the same stop.

Make it a habit: photograph the cooler fins at every maintenance stop. A series of photos shows how fast flux builds up and lets you set the cleaning interval from evidence instead of the calendar alone.

ARTICLE 05Conformal Coating Jigs Without Scraping

A recorded trial removed UV adhesive from aluminium jigs by spray.

Full articles are in English. The summary above carries the argument in your language.

Seven sets of aluminium coating jigs sit on a bench, each edge coated with cured UV adhesive. The usual tool is a scraper and an afternoon, and every pass risks a burr on a sealing face.

Cured adhesive and coating overspray build up on masking jigs until parts no longer sit flat and coating creeps where it should not. Scraping is slow, uneven and hard on soft aluminium.

The method: run a small trial before you change the routine. Choose a water-based agent for your residue, fix temperature and time, rinse, dry and inspect - and only then write the settings into the work instruction.

Make it a habit: keep the trial settings with a photo of the cleaned jig as the reference. When a new coating or adhesive arrives, repeat the trial instead of guessing.

ARTICLE 07Pressure Tells You the Filter Is Full

Watching wash and rinse pressure turns surprises into routine maintenance.

Full articles are in English. The summary above carries the argument in your language.

Halfway through a load the cleaning result falls off: pockets still brown, the spray looks weak. Nobody changed a setting. The filter core has been clogging for a week.

Spray cleaning depends on flow and pressure at the nozzles. A clogged filter or nozzle reduces both gradually, so parts come out slightly worse every day until someone notices residue - by then a batch has to be repeated.

The method: treat pressure as the health signal of any spray system. Know the normal value, set a window around it and act when it leaves the window - before the result on the parts changes.

The SME5200 has pressure sensors on the wash and rinse lines that alarm when pressure leaves its set window - the first sign of a clogged filter or nozzle. Behind them sit the pump-protection screen, the particle screen and the 20 µm filter core, all reachable in the filter bucket.

Make it a habit: write down the wash and rinse pressure at the start of each week. A slow trend shows when the next filter change is due, and an alarm becomes a planned job instead of a bad batch.
