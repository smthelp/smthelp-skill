The Line-Balance Reality

One blocked machine stops the whole line — every time

Without a buffer, your fastest placement machine runs at the speed of your slowest neighbor, and every AOI stop or reflow alarm becomes a line-wide stoppage.

Cascade stoppages

AOI pauses for five minutes and the placement machine behind it starves, the printer ahead of it backs up — the entire line pays for one machine's break.

Hot boards, no place to go

Reflow output lands on an open conveyor with nowhere to queue, so operators hand-carry solder-hot boards to whatever flat surface is free.

NG boards blocking OK flow

Without NG/OK lanes, a rejected board physically blocks good product behind it until an operator walks over and clears it.

Changeover dead time

Loader swaps and magazine changes empty the line segment every shift — minutes of throughput lost at every product switch.

Cycle-time mismatch

A 12-second placer feeding an 18-second AOI leaves gaps all day — the difference is pure lost capacity, shift after shift.

Hand-carry damage

Every manual board transfer between machines is a scratch, a dropped panel and an ESD event waiting for an audit to find.

Decouple · Store · Sort · Release

How a buffer buys back line speed

The buffer sits between two machines and absorbs the difference — boards flow in, queue up, sort out and release on SMEMA handshake without an operator touching anything.

STEP 1 · RECEIVE Boards enter on SMEMA handshake — the upstream machine never learns the downstream one is busy
STEP 2 · STORE Lift, stack or magazine-queue up to 20 boards per lane in six selectable operation modes
STEP 3 · SORT NG/OK lanes divert rejected boards automatically — good product never waits behind a reject
STEP 4 · RELEASE When the downstream machine is ready, the buffer feeds at line speed and the queue drains itself

Four Configurations

Pick the buffer that matches your bottleneck — or mix them

All four speak SMEMA and match standard 900 mm line height. Choose magazine type for loader/AOI duty, NG/OK dual-track for inspection output, single-track cooling for reflow exit, or customized buffers built to your layout.

Type A · EDM300 / EDM330 / EDM360 · magazine type buffer

The flagship buffer: queues boards in standard magazines with top and bottom pneumatic clamps, LED membrane panel, six operation modes — SMEMA compatible across the range.

Feature

Three chassis sizes (EDM300/330/360) handling PCBs from 80 × 50 up to 500 × 280 mm and magazines to 600 × 400 mm; six operation modes; pneumatic magazine clamps; special gas circuit guarantees no PCB breakage; MD board support for narrow PCBs.

Advantage

Magazine storage means true high-capacity buffering — and the sealed gas circuit moves boards without the mechanical shock that cracks solder joints on heavy boards.

Benefit

Your loader or AOI can stop for a full changeover while placement keeps running — throughput stays at machine speed instead of dropping to zero.

Type B · BC-M / BC-L / BC-XL-TN · S-type magazine buffer

The multi-function workhorse: pusher-type board handling into S-type magazines, rated for AOI, SMT and NG/OK duty with 20 boards per cycle.

Three footprints — BC-M-TN (1000 × 710 × 1170 mm), BC-L-TN (1200 × 800 × 1670 mm), BC-XL-TN (1300 × 900 × 1750 mm); universal 120–250 V supply; 250–380 W; NG/OK buffering of 20 boards; supports AOI and SMT line duty.

Advantage

Three chassis sizes scale the buffer to the floor space you actually have — from compact line-side units to full-height high-capacity buffering behind AOI.

Type C · 825 / 833 / 839 / 846 · post-reflow cooling buffer

The reflow exit specialist: single or dual track cooling buffers that queue hot boards as they leave the oven — no more hand-carrying solder-hot panels.

Four lengths from 950 to 1500 mm (models 825/833/839/846), single or dual track, 100 W low-power drive; buffers 20 boards while they cool on the track.

Benefit

The inspection gatekeeper: dual-track buffers after SPI and AOI that split good boards from rejects automatically, with fully customizable builds.

Benefit

FAQ

Before you order

How many boards do I need to buffer?

Rule of thumb: enough boards to cover your longest routine downstream stop. A 5-minute AOI stop on a 12-second cycle needs about 25 boards of storage. Send your cycle time and stop profile and we size the buffer with real numbers.

Will it talk to my existing machines?

Yes — all buffers in this series are SMEMA compatible and match standard 900 mm transport height (adjustable). Tell us the two neighboring machine models and we confirm the handshake and height before quoting.

Magazine type or track type — which do I need?

Magazine buffers (Type A/B) hold the most boards in the smallest footprint and suit loader, unloader and AOI positions. Track buffers (Type C/D) suit reflow exit and NG/OK sorting where boards keep moving on belts. Most lines use both.

Can boards cool properly on a track buffer?

Type C cooling buffers add queue time as cooling time — the four lengths (950–1500 mm) set how long boards ride before handling. For thick, high-mass boards we size the length so exit temperature hits your handling spec.

Are custom layouts possible?

Yes. Track count, lane width, transport height, direction and control logic are all built to order — send a line sketch and the boards you handle, and we quote the non-standard build.

How do I get a quote?

Email info@smthelp.com or message us on WhatsApp with three things: board size, the two machines the buffer sits between, and your line height. Quotes go out within 24 hours on working days.
