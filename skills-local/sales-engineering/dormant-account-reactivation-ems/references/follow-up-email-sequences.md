# Follow-Up Email Sequences — Dormant EMS Account Reactivation

## Core Principle

Round 1 (first email) = pain共鸣 + value offer.  
Round 2 (3-5 days no reply) = **稀缺性/紧迫感** hook (subsidy deadline, window closing).  
Round 3 (7-10 days no reply) = **竞争对手FOMO** hook (other shops in region already automating).

Never repeat the same hook angle in consecutive rounds. Each round must feel like a different conversation.

## Round 2 (No Reply, Days 3-5) — Hook: Government Subsidy Scarcity

**Subject:** Did you catch the Bulgarian automation grant deadline? — [Customer] THT upgrade

**Logic:** Most procurement managers have seen 5-10 generic automation pitches. The subsidy angle is time-sensitive and specific — it creates urgency without being a hard sell.

**Body:**
```
Hi [Name],

Gentle follow-up — just wanted to flag something time-sensitive.

[Bulgaria/Poland/ROMANIA's] Recovery & Resilience Plan offers up to 50% CapEx 
subsidy for Industry 4.0 automation projects. The current funding window closes 
[INSERT ACTUAL DATE — verify before sending], and several [region] EMS shops 
are already in application.

If [Customer] is even considering a THT upgrade, locking in the subsidy before 
the window closes could cut your net investment in half.

I've attached a one-page summary: Which machines qualify, what the application 
timeline looks like, and how Southern Machinery's EtherCAT-compatible equipment 
meets the technical requirements.

No pressure. But if this is worth exploring, I'm happy to schedule a 20-min 
call this week to walk through the numbers.

Best,
[Jason Wu]
WhatsApp: +86 136 0256 2576
www.SMThelp.com
```

**Key rules:**
- ALWAYS verify the actual grant deadline before sending. Bulgaria RRP had multiple calls; the most recent Izbor 1 deadline was 2025 — check if a new one is open.
- If no verifiable deadline exists, use a softer angle: "Several companies in your region are actively applying" — don't fabricate a fake cutoff date.

## Round 3 (No Reply, Days 7-10) — Hook: Competitor FOMO

**Subject:** [Competitor EMS company in same region] just automated their THT line — here's what they chose

**Logic:** FOMO is the strongest late-stage re-engagement trigger. Naming a real competitor in the same country creates immediate relevance.

**Body:**
```
Hi [Name],

Third attempt — hope I'm not overstaying my welcome.

Quick data point: a mid-size EMS shop in [BULGARIA/POLAND/REGION] just 
completed their THT automation upgrade with Southern Machinery. They were running 
the same manual DIP setup as [Customer] — 8-10 operators, similar board mix, 
similar labor costs.

Their result after 6 months of operation:
- 7 operators reduced to 1 per shift
- First-pass yield: 92% → 99.2%
- Payback: 7 months (without any government subsidy)

I'm not sharing this to pressure you. But if [Customer] is still running 
manual DIP while competitors are automating, the cost gap widens every month.

I can share the full case study off-record if helpful. Or if the timing 
isn't right, just say the word and I'll check back in Q4.

Best,
[Jason Wu]
WhatsApp: +86 136 0256 2576
www.SMThelp.com
```

**Key rules:**
- Only use when you have a real (verified) regional reference case.
- Do NOT name a competitor without consent — frame as "a shop in [region]" if you cannot confirm the case is shareable.
- This template should NOT be used in Germany/Austria/Switzerland due to strict competitor reference restrictions.

## Key Pitfalls

- NEVER fabricate a grant deadline to create false urgency — this destroys credibility in EU markets
- NEVER use Round 2 (subsidy) + Round 3 (competitor) in the same sequence without a real data point
- WhatsApp messages under 60 words for European B2B buyers — they scan, not read
- For Poland/Czechia: English-only WhatsApp is fine (technical buyers prefer it)
- For LATAM: always open with 1-2 words in local language, then English for the technical content
