# Feel-Felt-Found Objection Handling — EMS Equipment Sales

## Framework

| Stage | Purpose | Content Rule |
|-------|---------|-------------|
| **Feel** | 100% empathy — never contradict | Match their emotion exactly, show understanding |
| **Felt** | Bridge — other clients in same situation | Tell a specific, credible story |
| **Found** | Redirect to solution | Show the path forward without dismissing their concern |

**Golden rules:**
- Never use the word "but" — replace with "and" or "what I have found is"
- Never argue, defend, or explain why they're wrong
- Each stage is 1-3 sentences max — be brief
- End with a question that re-engages, not a statement

## Objection 1: "We already use Panasonic/Universal equipment"

### Feel:
> I completely understand — Panasonic and Universal have been industry standards for decades. They make excellent SMT placement machines, and your team has clearly invested time in mastering them.

### Felt:
> What we've heard from several clients in [region] is that their Panasonic and Universal lines are exceptional for SMT placement — but when it comes to the THT/DIP section, those machines were not designed for odd-form insertion. Connectors, relays, transformers — these components require a different machine architecture. Many clients end up with a hybrid setup: the Panasonic handles SMD, and then they add separate equipment for the THT components. That actually creates more complexity, not less.

> One client in Romania was running a Panasonic NPM line with 8 manual DIP operators. They were spending €90,000 per year on labor for that one section alone. When they added a single S-70LD from Southern Machinery alongside their existing line, they cut it to 1 operator monitoring the machine. Paid back in under 8 months.

### Found:
> Here's what we typically suggest: you don't need to replace Panasonic or Universal. You add Southern Machinery specifically for the THT/DIP section where those brands cannot serve you. Think of it as a targeted upgrade, not a full line swap.

> On the budget concern — with the [Bulgaria/Poland] government automation grant at 50% CapEx, your net investment on an S-70LD could be cut in half. And our machines are designed to work alongside existing equipment without re-engineering your floor layout.

### Close:
> Would it help if I put together a side-by-side comparison showing exactly where the Panasonic ends and where the Southern Machinery THT solution begins — and what the ROI looks like with and without the subsidy?

### Feel:

We've heard this from clients who bought a "Universal-compatible" machine from an unverified source — it worked for 6 months, then the spare parts supply dried up.

And we support clients in 50+ countries with 24-48 hour remote service response. The difference is not the country of origin — it's the engineering discipline behind the machine.

### Found:
> For your specific concern, I can arrange a live video factory tour of our Shenzhen facility — you can see the assembly line, the testing process, and the spare parts warehouse. Or I can connect you with 2-3 reference clients in [region] who have been running Southern Machinery equipment for 3+ years. Either way, you get unfiltered data before you make any decision.

### Close:
> Would a reference call with a client in [same country] be more useful than a datasheet?

## General Feel-Felt-Found Rules

1. **Feel must be genuine** — if you don't actually understand their concern, ask a clarifying question first: "Can you tell me more about what's driving that concern?"  
2. **Felt needs a specific number** — vague "other clients felt the same way" is weak. Use: "One client in [city] was spending €X/year on..."
3. **Found must always redirect to a next step** — the goal is not to win the argument, it's to get them to take one micro-action (reply, open a link, accept a call)
4. **Use the word "and" not "but"** — "I understand your concern AND what I've found is..."
5. **Match their language** — if they say "we're happy with our current setup," don't say "actually you're not" — say "That's exactly why Southern Machinery fits — we complement your existing setup rather than replace it."
