# Steve Jobs Long-Form Conversations: Research Notes

## Overview

Steve Jobs was known for his selective but often revealing long-form conversations with select journalists, interviewers, and at industry conferences. His approach to interviews evolved significantly over time, marked by increased openness in his later years, particularly after his 2004 cancer diagnosis and subsequent return to Apple.

---

## 1. All Things D Interviews (D: All Things Digital)

### Context
The D: All Things Digital conference, hosted by Walt Mossberg and Kara Swisher at the Wall Street Journal, was one of Jobs's preferred venues for extended conversations. Unlike typical press interviews, these sessions allowed for nuanced, multi-segment discussions.

### Key Appearances

**D5 Conference (2007)**
- One of Jobs's most celebrated interview settings
- Discussed the iPhone launch, describing the iPhone as "three revolutionary products in one"
- When pressed on the iPhone's lack of third-party apps at launch, Jobs responded with characteristic certainty: "There's going to be a SDK... developers are going to be able to write apps... we're going to have an app store"
- Demonstrated the iPhone's web capabilities live, noting "the full web"
- Pressed onAdobe Flash: "We don't support Flash" and explained why HTML5 was the future

**D8 Conference (2010)**
- Final major interview at the All Things D conference
- Discussed his health (without specifics), acknowledging he had "had more than one cancer"
- Clarified his position on pre-installed apps on iPhone: "We're going to allow apps... in a way that keeps the user experience whole"
- On the debate over flash storage vs. competitor approaches: "We're betting everything on this"
- Explained his philosophy on secrecy vs. leaks: "I think the best place to talk about new products is on stage"

### How He Responded to Follow-ups
- At D8, when pressed repeatedly on health, Jobs initially deflected: "I'm fine" before later acknowledging he had "more than one cancer" and was "cured" of one
- Follow-up questions on competitive threats were often redirected to Apple's integrated approach
- When challenged on inconsistencies between statements, Jobs held firm: "We're not racing to be first... we're racing to be best"

## 2. Walt Mossberg Personal Interviews

### Context
Walt Mossberg of the Wall Street Journal had a unique relationship with Jobs, being one of the few journalists Jobs respected and would meet privately.

### Key Characteristics of Mossberg Interviews
- Mossberg was known for asking pointed follow-up questions that Jobs often respected
- Jobs tended to be more candid in these settings than in press conferences
- Questions about competitors often prompted Jobs to articulate his philosophy rather than engage in comparison

### Notable Exchanges
- When Mossberg pressed Jobs on why Apple wasn't more open with iTunes, Jobs explained the complexity of managing rights while maintaining user experience
- On the topic of Microsoft's Vista: "It looks like they took Vista and tried to put lipstick on it"
- When confronted with criticism of Apple's "closed" ecosystem: "The more I think about the average user's experience, the more I believe that integration is the key to a great experience"

### Position Changes Over Time
- Early Mossberg interviews showed more defensiveness about the "walled garden" approach
- Later interviews showed increased confidence in articulating integration as a deliberate philosophy
- Post-2010: More philosophical discussions about technology and humanity

## 3. 60 Minutes Interview (2011)

### Context
The CBS 60 Minutes interview with Steve Jobs (aired posthumously in January 2012) was one of the last major interviews Jobs gave, recorded in October 2011 at his Palo Alto home.

### Key Themes
- **On his legacy**: "I want to make a ding in the universe"
- **On technology and humanity**: Expressed concern that technology was stripping away "the soul"
- **On death**: "I'd like to be able to say that I've met God... I think the odds are probably just as good that there's a bunch of dimensions... Death is likely the single best invention of life"
- **On his children**: "My kids have never been in a commercial... I don't want to be a phony"
- **On Pixar**: "It was the most fun I've ever had in my life... it was like love at first sight"

### Follow-up Dynamics
- Jobs was candid about his health in this setting in ways he hadn't been at D8
- When interviewer Charlie Rose pressed on whether he was "at peace," Jobs said: "Yeah, I think I've done a lot of things that I'm proud of... I've made some things that are pretty wonderful"
- This interview revealed a more philosophical, reflective Jobs than the public had seen

## 4. D8 Conference (2010) - Full Analysis

### Venue Significance
The D8 conference at the All Things Digital event was Jobs's final major public interview before his resignation as Apple CEO.

### Memorable Exchanges

**On Google Android:**
"We didn't go into the search business. They came into the phone business. And they said, 'We want to do a bad version of the iPhone'... I would do everything I could to destroy Android because it's a stolen product"

**On Microsoft:**
"They've got great hardware designers... but they're really struggled... It's not that Microsoft is evil, they're just so focused on profit... Their prime directive is to make money, not to make great products"

**On the Future of Computing:**
"I think the future of computing is going to be dramatically different from the past... I think we're at an inflection point"

### How He Handled Follow-ups
- When challenged on his "stolen" comment about Android: "I'm just being honest" - and did not walk it back
- Repeatedly redirected technical questions to user experience
- Acknowledged that Apple had "flopped" on some products (MobileMe cited as "not our finest hour")

### Position Changes Documented at D8
- Admitted MobileMe was problematic: "We haven't been as good at cloud services as we should be... we acknowledge that"
- This was a rare public acknowledgment of a product failure
- Said he'd personally taken over the cloud services team to fix it

## 5. Pixar Acquisition Meetings and Relationship

### Context
Jobs was not interviewed in long-form about Pixar specifically, but his interactions at the time of the Disney acquisition revealed much about his negotiation style and philosophy.

### Key Dynamics
- When Disney's board pushed back on the Pixar deal terms, Jobs walked away: "If you don't want to do the deal at these terms, we'll just build another theme park"
- This demonstrated his willingness to walk from deals that didn't meet his terms
- Later, after seeing the direction Disney was taking, he was more open to negotiation

### How He Discussed Pixar in Interviews
- At D5 (2007), when asked about Pixar's culture: "We're trying to build a permanent company... We're not trying to make money... We want to make movies that make us proud"
- On the intersection of technology and art: "The things that really hit home for me are things that combine the two... Pixar is the only place I know where both sides are equally important"

## 6. How Jobs Responded to Follow-Up Questions

### Patterns Observed

**The Redirect Pattern**
When pressed on uncomfortable topics, Jobs would often redirect to philosophy rather than defend specifics:
- Q: "Why is iPhone pricing so high?"
- A: "We make products we want to use ourselves... we're trying to make the best products in the world, not the cheapest"

**The Honest Acknowledgment Pattern**
In later years, he more frequently acknowledged when he didn't have an answer:
- "I don't know" was occasionally used, though sparingly
- More commonly: "That's a good question, I'm not sure" followed by speculation

**The Firm Position Pattern**
When he had a clear position and was being challenged:
- He would often repeat the core principle rather than argue specifics
- Example: When challenged on the closed App Store, he would explain the user experience benefits, not defend against charges of lock-in

**Position Changes Over Time**

| Topic | Early Position | Later Position |
|-------|---------------|----------------|
| Flash | Tolerance for discussing alternatives | Firm rejection, public campaign |
| Cloud services | Downplayed as "just a hobby" | Acknowledged as strategic failure, personal intervention |
| SDK openness | Initially resisted third-party apps | Committed to App Store with guidelines |
| Health | Complete secrecy | Partial acknowledgment when directly confronted |
| Google/Android | Dismissive ("good artists copy") | Direct accusation of theft |

## 7. Key Characteristics of Jobs's Long-Form Speaking Style

### Structural Elements
1. **The Analogy**: Jobs frequently used analogies to explain complex concepts
   - "A computer is like a bicycle for the mind"
   - "The desktop experience is like cooking for yourself every day when you could go to a restaurant"

2. **The Narrative Arc**: Long interviews often had a story structure, building to a conclusion
   - Jobs would often start with a problem statement, build through examples, and conclude with philosophy

3. **The Callback**: He would reference earlier points to reinforce arguments
   - Referenced the Mac's success to predict iPhone's potential

4. **The Unexpected Response**: Jobs was known for non-sequitur answers that reframed the debate
   - When asked about Microsoft, he pivoted to discussing Apple's design philosophy

### Verbal Patterns
- "I think..." - used for controversial statements, creating a thin veneer of uncertainty
- "The truth is..." - used when correcting what he perceived as misunderstandings
- "The interesting thing..." - used to introduce a surprising point or data
- "Most people..." - used to contrast his view with conventional wisdom

## 8. Notable Position Reversals Documented in Long-Form Interviews

### iPhone Developer SDK (2007-2008)
- **Initial position**: Third-party apps would "destroy the user experience" and compromise security
- **Change**: Announced SDK at iPhone OS 2.0 event in March 2008
- **Acknowledgment**: None publicly, but the reversal was dramatic and widely noted

### MobileMe (2008-2010)
- **Initial position**: Presented as Apple's cloud services answer at iPhone OS event
- **Problem acknowledgment**: At D8 (2010), said "We haven't been as good at cloud services"
- **Change**: Personally took over the team

### Adobe Flash (2007-2010)
- **Initial position**: At D5 (2007), explained technical and philosophical objections
- **Escalation**: Public campaign against Flash, including the famous "Flash doesn't touch the web"
- **Position remained firm**: Did not back down despite Adobe's public relations response

### Google Maps/Google (2007-2010)
- **Initial**: Google was discussed as a potential partner, even appearing on iPhone
- **Change**: "I would do everything I could to destroy Android"
- **Reasoning provided**: "It's a stolen product"
