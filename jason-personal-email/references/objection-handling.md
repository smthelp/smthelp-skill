# Objection Handling — Detailed Feel-Felt-Found Scripts

## The Framework

**Feel** — Acknowledge the objection as valid and common  
**Felt** — Reference other factories that felt the same way  
**Found** — Share what those factories discovered / how they solved it  

---

## Objection 1: "Your equipment quality is not as good as Japanese brands"

**Why it works:** Validates the prospect's concern without being defensive. 
Moves from "us vs. them" to "here's what others found."

**Script:**
> I understand that concern — it's actually the first thing most buyers ask us. 
> What we've found is that for 80% of SMT applications, the production output 
> difference is imperceptible between well-engineered Chinese equipment and Japanese 
> OEM lines. The gap becomes real in ultra-high-precision applications where 
> sub-0.1mm accuracy matters.
>
> Other factories we work with — especially in Mexico and Vietnam — had the same 
> hesitation initially. What changed their mind was the 30-day OEE performance 
> guarantee we offer: if the machine doesn't perform, you don't pay.
>
> Would a 30-day performance bond help address that concern?

**Pivot to:** ROI calculation or customer reference call

---

## Objection 2: "We tried Chinese equipment before and it failed"

**Why it works:** Validates the specific bad experience, doesn't dismiss it.

**Script:**
> I understand — the market has a lot of low-quality players, and bad 
> experiences create lasting impressions. That's fair.
>
> The difference I'd point out is in the support infrastructure: we have 
> dedicated field engineers (not contractors), 7×24 remote diagnostics, 
> and we guarantee spare parts availability. When something breaks, we 
> show up — that's the difference between us and the $5,000 machine that 
> worked for 6 months and then became a doorstop.
>
> Would it help if I connected you with one of our existing customers in 
> [their region] to hear about their 12-month experience firsthand?

**Pivot to:** Customer reference call, then ROI model

---

## Objection 3: "We don't have budget right now"

**Why it works:** Doesn't fight the objection — uses it to start building 
the business case for later.

**Script:**
> Totally understand — CapEx cycles can be long for capital equipment, 
> especially for foreign currency purchases.
>
> Here's what I'd suggest: let me put together a rough ROI model so you 
> have it ready when the next budget window opens. Most factories find 
> the numbers make the internal case much easier — particularly when you 
> can show the payback period and compare it to the cost of doing nothing.
>
> I can have a draft to you within 24 hours. What email should I send it to?

**Pivot to:** ROI document + follow-up in 2 weeks

---

## Objection 4: "Your delivery time is too long"

**Why it works:** Reframes the comparison — 4 weeks vs. 6-9 months.

**Script:**
> I want to make sure I'm comparing apples to apples: our 4-week delivery 
> is for a fully configured, CE-certified machine with custom specifications.
>
> Japanese OEM quotes for comparable equipment typically run 6-9 months. 
> Even the standard Chinese suppliers I see quoted run 8-12 weeks.
>
> If you have a tighter deadline, let's talk — I can check our current 
> factory load and see if a fast-track option exists.

**Pivot to:** Explore timeline flexibility, remote commissioning option

---

## Objection 5: "The price is higher than I expected"

**Why it works:** Anchors on value and ROI, not on the sticker price.

**Script:**
> I understand — sticker price is always surprising before you see the math.
>
> Here's what factories in your situation typically tell me after 12 months: 
> the ROI calculation changes how you look at the number. At a 14-month 
> payback on a machine with a 5-year service life, the math works — particularly 
> when you factor in the labor savings and OEE improvement.
>
> What I can't do is reduce quality, support, or warranty terms — that's 
> where the real value lives for you.

**Pivot to:** ROI model with specific numbers

---

## Objection 6: "I need to talk to my team / manager / boss"

**Why it works:** Acknowledges the buying process, provides tools to help them internal sell.

**Script:**
> Of course — capital equipment decisions rarely happen unilaterally.
>
> Here's what I'd suggest: let me put together a one-pager that you can 
> share with your team — covering the ROI, the comparison to Japanese 
> alternatives, and the support guarantee. That way, when you bring it 
> to them, you're bringing a complete picture, not just a price.
>
> What's the best format for your team — PDF, or a shared document?

**Pivot to:** One-pager + meeting to present to their team

---

## Objection 7: "We're already working with [competitor]"

**Why it works:** Acknowledges the competitor relationship without disparaging them.

**Script:**
> I understand — building a relationship with an equipment supplier takes time.
>
> Here's what I'd offer: no pressure to switch. But what I'd suggest is 
> keeping our ROI model on file — particularly if your current supplier's 
> lead times or support response ever change. The market for SMT automation 
> is shifting, and it helps to have a backup option that's already vetted.
>
> Can I send you our standard one-pager for your records?

**Pivot to:** Keep in nurture list, follow up in 90 days

---

## Objection 8: "I don't have time for a call"

**Why it works:** Respects their time, offers minimal-friction alternatives.

**Script:**
> I completely understand — you're running production, not evaluating equipment.
>
> How about this: send me your current bottleneck in one line — just tell me 
> what keeps you up at night about the line — and I'll send you a written 
> recommendation. No call required unless something in it interests you.

**Pivot to:** Written recommendation + follow-up based on their response
