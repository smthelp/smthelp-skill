### II. PROBLEM DEFINITION (300-400 words)

**What Makes Loose Terminals Hard to Automate**

Unlike tape-and-reel components (where a feeder peel-and-places each part with mechanical precision), loose bulk components arrive in an unorganized heap. The automation challenge has three layers:

1. **Orientation chaos** — Terminal blocks and hardware parts land in random orientations in the vibratory bowl. A feeder must sort them into a single, predictable orientation — typically via a angled track with physical barriers and optical sensors — before they reach the pick-up position.

2. **Shape variety** — A bag of "terminal blocks" might contain 8-12 different SKUs varying in height (±15mm), width, and lead configuration. Traditional feeders require full reconfiguration for each SKU.

3. **Integration complexity** — The feeder must deliver one part, precisely oriented, at the exact moment the insertion machine's head arrives — with ±0.5mm positional accuracy and sub-second cycle time. Any mismatch between feeder speed and inserter throughput creates a bottleneck.

**Why Manual insertion is the false economy**

### II. PROBLEM DEFINITION (300-400 words)

**The Hidden Cost Structure of Wave Soldering**

Wave soldering has four major cost drivers that rarely appear on the original equipment quote:

**1. Energy Consumption**
The solder pot must be maintained at 250-265°C continuously during operation, plus preheat zones (150-200°C across 4 IR zones), plus the conveyor motor, flux application system, and cooling fans. Older single-wave machines consume **32-48 kW/h** continuously. The S-WS350B's optimized heating system draws **28-32 kW/h** at full load.

**2. Nitrogen Consumption**
Lead-free soldering requires an oxygen-reduced atmosphere to prevent oxidation and improve wetting. Standard systems consume **15-25 m³/h** of liquid nitrogen. The S-WS350B's nitrogen-saver mode reduces this to **8-12 m³/h** — a 40% reduction that significantly lowers consumable cost.

**3. Solder Dross Formation**
SAC305 lead-free solder forms tin oxide dross at a rate approximately **2-3x faster** than tin-lead solder. A typical wave solder machine operating 8 hours/day generates **8-15 kg of dross per week** — at $28-35/kg for SAC305, that's **$224-525/week in dross loss alone**. The S-WS350B's titanium wave fingers and optimized wave geometry reduce dross formation by approximately **30%**.

**4. Operator Intervention Time**
Non-PC-controlled systems require manual adjustment of wave height, conveyor speed, and temperature during product changeover. This typically costs **20-45 minutes per changeover** at operator labor rates. The S-WS350B's PC-controlled system stores **200+ process recipes** with one-touch recall, reducing changeover to **5-10 minutes**.

**Total hidden hourly cost difference:** Plants running older single-wave systems versus the S-WS350B typically see **$31-47/hour in additional operating costs** that go untracked.
